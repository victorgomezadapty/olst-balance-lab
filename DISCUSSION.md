# Discussion

## What the numbers say

On the same cued 4 s yoga tree task, the clearest age difference in this healthy sample is consistency:
younger adults almost never needed a retry (1.9% of windows), older adults did in about 28% of windows, with
large differences between older individuals (0% to 86%). The first stable hold in a window was about 0.8 s
shorter in the older group, but the best hold an older adult produced in any short window was as long as
the younger adults'. A reasonable clinical reading, limited to this task and sample, is that many healthy
older adults can produce a full short hold but do not do it reliably on the first attempt.

The time to stability result runs the other way (older adults reached the labelled start of stability
sooner). The label is derived from the lifted knee angle settling. The force-plate check shows that the
stance foot centre of pressure keeps settling for about 1.3 s after that label in both groups, so this
endpoint describes how quickly the lifted foot reaches its position, not how quickly sway settles. Older
participants may also have placed the foot lower (the protocol allowed the calf), which would shorten the
knee movement; the deposit does not record foot placement, so this stays a hypothesis.

## Limitations

* **Task mismatch.** Yoga tree pose, arms overhead, cued 4 s windows with retries. This is not the clinical
  one-leg stand or unipedal stance test, so no hold time here can be compared with clinical norms or cut offs.
* **Ceiling.** In 4 s windows the stable hold cannot go much beyond about 3.5 s; younger adults sit at that
  ceiling, which compresses differences.
* **The end cue.** A present `t_break` usually marks the end cue, not a loss of balance. The window failure
  rule (retry or never stable) avoids this, but it depends on assigning attempts to windows from timing,
  because cue times are not in the deposit. The rule agreed with a simple time band rule for 773 of 784 short
  attempts.
* **Censoring.** 237 attempts end at the recording end. The primary hold summary includes them as observed;
  removing those windows (S5) does not change the conclusion.
* **Healthy volunteers only,** two age groups with a 32 year gap and no middle aged adults. No falls history,
  no clinical population, no follow up. This is not fall risk evidence.
* **Unbalanced design.** Long 20 s windows exist only for the Old group, and 43 extra fourth captures exist
  only for Old participants (removing them, S6, does not change the conclusion).
* **Uneven attempt counts.** People with poorer balance contribute more and shorter attempts; this is why the
  analysis summarises per person first.
* **Small groups** (15 and 17). Intervals are wide for the older group, and the self rating correlations are
  descriptive only.
* **Leg dominance was not recorded,** so left vs right base is not dominant vs non dominant.
* **Force-plate check.** The simple COP rules were fixed in advance and are crude; their disagreement with
  the labels is informative about what the labels measure, not a verdict that the labels are wrong.

## Short literature note (open abstracts only)

* Jonsson, Seiger and Hirschfeld (2004) described a dynamic phase of rapidly falling force variability in the
  first seconds of one-leg stance, then a static phase, with older adults reducing variability less in the
  first 5 s. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinbiomech.2004.04.002. The 1.3 s lag between the knee based
  stability label and the COP based onset seen here is consistent with sway still settling after the posture
  is reached.
* Springer et al. (2007) give age specific normative values for the unipedal stance test with eyes open and
  closed (arms crossed, up to 45 s, best of three). https://doi.org/10.1519/00139143-200704000-00003. Those
  norms are for a different task and are not applied here.
* Vellas et al. (1997) linked inability to stand 5 s on one leg with injurious falls in community living
  older adults. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-5415.1997.tb01479.x
* Araujo et al. (2022) linked inability to complete a 10 s one-legged stance with mortality in middle aged
  and older adults. https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105360
* Blodgett et al. (2022) systematically reviewed one-legged balance and falls in community dwelling adults.
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arr.2021.101501

None of these studies used the yoga tree pose with arms overhead in cued 4 s windows, so they give context,
not a benchmark.

## What remains to investigate

* COP sway measures (path length, area, frequency) in the stable phase on the force plates already downloaded,
  by age group, with a pre-specified plan.
* Time from foot lift to a force-plate based onset, to separate foot placement time from sway settling.
* Whether retries cluster in the first window of a capture (learning or warm up) or in later captures (fatigue).
* The long 20 s windows: a time to first touchdown analysis with censoring, Old only.
* Agreement of radar based features with the labels, which this project does not attempt.
* A replication in a sample with middle aged adults, a clinical population and the standard clinical test
  alongside the yoga tree task.
