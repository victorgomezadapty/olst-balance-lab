# Portfolio brief (locked facts)

Language: English by default; a Spanish version can be prepared later. Nothing here is published.

## One line

An independent, pre-specified re-analysis of an open PhysioNet one-leg balance deposit: on the same cued 4 s
yoga tree task, healthy older adults needed a retry or never settled in about 28% of windows vs 1.9% for
younger adults, while their best hold matched.

## Locked facts

* Data: PhysioNet `olst-mocap-forceplate-radar` 1.0, DOI 10.13026/46hn-6b25, CC BY 4.0, Copeland, Linton,
  Zhang, Lugaro, Mori and Anthony (2026). Also cite PhysioNet (Pollard et al. 2026,
  https://doi.org/10.1038/s44360-026-00096-z).
* 32 healthy adults: 15 aged 19 to 32, 17 aged 64 to 78; 1,241 labelled attempts in 334 captures.
* Primary sample: all short captures, 267 Young and 362 Old cued windows, one summary per person.
* Window failure: Young 1.9% (0.4% to 3.8%), Old 28.0% (17.5% to 40.2%), +26.0 points (+15.4 to +38.4).
* Time to stability: Young 0.59 s, Old 0.34 s, -0.25 s (-0.33 to -0.17); a knee angle label, not sway.
* First attempt stable hold: Young 3.35 s, Old 2.52 s, -0.83 s (-1.23 to -0.46).
* Best hold of any short attempt: no age difference (+0.18 s, -0.18 to +0.54).
* Force plates: 418 short capture files verified; lifted foot unloaded in 100% of labelled stable phases;
  COP based onset a median 1.3 s after the label.
* Audit: the missing capture for participant 12 is V1 in the files, not V2 as the README says; 43 extra V4
  captures (Old); 20 captures without a final attempt flag; out of date citation line.

## Must always be said

* The task is a yoga tree pose with arms overhead in cued windows, not the clinical one-leg stand test.
* Healthy adults only, no middle aged group; not fall risk diagnosis and not a clinical cut off.
* The companion paper is cited only (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0); none of its text, tables or figures are reused.
* The creators, PhysioNet and MIT do not endorse this work.
